## [GPT-6 Astra Demonstrates Advanced Robot Arm Control](https://openai.robocurve.org/gpt-6-astra/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

GPT-6 Astra, a limited preview released on September 3, 2026, has demonstrated advanced capabilities in controlling robot arms, outperforming previous models in tasks like block-into-bowl. In interleaved blinded tests, it achieved 19/20 success compared to Claude Fable 5.1's 8/20, while using 80% fewer output tokens. This marks a significant step in AI and robotics integration, suggesting that large language models can effectively handle physical tasks, which could accelerate the development of real-world robotics applications like automated trash pickup. The improved efficiency and lower cost also make such systems more commercially viable. GPT-6 Astra scored 95% on a robot control task, up from Fable 5.1's 40%, with 6.2x fewer output tokens at 2.3x lower cost. However, on harder precision tasks, both models had the same success rate (2/20), though Astra used 3.9x fewer tokens.

On September 5, 2025, Isar Aerospace's Spectrum rocket successfully reached orbit from Andøya Spaceport in Norway, marking the first orbital launch from Western European soil. This was the company's second launch attempt, following a failed first flight about 18 months earlier. This achievement is a historic milestone for European private spaceflight, demonstrating that Europe can launch satellites from its own soil without relying on overseas spaceports like French Guiana. It enhances European sovereignty and competitiveness in space access, potentially enabling higher launch cadence and lower costs for the region. The Spectrum rocket is a two-stage, liquid-fueled vehicle developed and largely manufactured in-house by Isar Aerospace, including its Aquila engines. It is designed to carry up to 1,000 kilograms to low Earth orbit and uses propane as fuel, with carbon composite structures.

## [Language Models Can Control Their Own Attention](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1w7sgf3/language_models_can_control_their_own_attention_r/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

This paper introduces Declarative Attention (DA), a protocol that allows language models to declare their attention modes (global, focus, local) within their chain-of-thought, enabling the inference engine to skip most KV cache reads. In zero-shot evaluations on 15 long-context tasks, DA reduced attended tokens by 52.0% on Gemma-4-31B and 31.1% on Qwen-3.6-27B, with modest accuracy drops of 1.27pp and 2.75pp respectively. This approach addresses a major bottleneck in long-context inference by reducing the computational cost of attention without requiring additional training or proxy models. It could enable more efficient deployment of LLMs for tasks involving very long contexts, such as document analysis or long conversations, and opens a new axis for sparse attention research. DA is a zero-shot protocol that works on off-the-shelf models, parsing the model's generated text to construct an attention mask without per-step selection overhead. The accuracy drop diminishes with model scale, suggesting potential for further gains with training-based methods.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/eigenlaplace · Sep 5, 06:07

**Background**: In autoregressive language models, the KV cache stores key and value vectors for previously generated tokens to avoid recomputation. However, during decoding, the model still attends to the entire context, reading all KV cache entries, which becomes costly for long contexts. Traditional sparse attention methods use proxy scores to pre-select relevant tokens, but these still incur O(N) overhead per step. DA instead asks the model itself to declare which parts of the context are relevant, eliminating the need for external scoring.
