## [OpenAI Claims Breakthrough on Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wavdi7/openal_says_it_has_cracked_one_of_maths/) ⭐️ 10.0/10

On September 8, 2026, OpenAI announced that an internal AI system had produced a proof showing that solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations can develop singularities in finite time, addressing the existence and smoothness problem. The proof was formalized in the Lean proof assistant, but has not yet been verified by external mathematicians or the Clay Mathematics Institute. If verified, this would be the first AI-discovered solution to a Millennium Prize Problem, potentially revolutionizing how mathematical research is conducted and demonstrating AI's capability to tackle deep theoretical challenges. It could also have profound implications for physics and engineering, where the Navier-Stokes equations are fundamental to understanding fluid dynamics and turbulence. The proof reportedly builds on a method developed by Diego Cordoba and Luis Martinez Zoroa in 2023 for proving blowup in related fluid equations. OpenAI stated it would decline the $1 million Millennium Prize if the result is officially recognized, and the announcement was accompanied by a priority dispute with researchers Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster, who had derived closely related results on the Euler equations.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Shizuka_Kuze · Sep 8, 17:42

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems set by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, each carrying a $1 million prize. It asks whether smooth, globally defined solutions always exist for the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations, which describe fluid motion; despite their widespread use, a rigorous mathematical understanding remains incomplete, particularly regarding turbulence. As of 2026, only the Poincaré conjecture had been officially solved, with Grigori Perelman declining the award.

## [Mathematician Accuses OpenAI of Suppressing Navier-Stokes Work](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Tristan Buckmaster, a mathematician at NYU's Courant Institute, published a statement claiming progress on Navier-Stokes-related problems and alleging that OpenAI attempted to suppress or co-opt his work, including pressuring him to drop his Anthropic-affiliated co-author. This dispute highlights the growing tension between academic research and corporate AI labs, raising critical questions about research credit, data usage, and ethics in the age of AI-assisted discovery. It could impact how mathematicians and scientists collaborate with AI companies. Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge claim to have proven finite-time blowup for several fluid equations, but not the full Clay Millennium Prize problem. OpenAI separately claimed an internal model proved a breakdown of Navier-Stokes solutions, leading to a priority dispute.

## [AlphaGenome Atlas: Predictive Map of Human DNA Variants](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/alphagenome-atlas/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Google DeepMind has released AlphaGenome Atlas, a comprehensive database predicting the molecular effects and AVI scores for 9 billion single-nucleotide variants across the human genome. This represents every possible single-letter DNA change. This resource could significantly accelerate genomics and medical research by enabling researchers to quickly assess the potential impact of genetic variants, aiding in the interpretation of sequencing data and the identification of disease-causing mutations. It democratizes access to high-quality predictive data, potentially impacting personalized medicine and genetic counseling. The Atlas includes predictions for 9 billion variants and provides AVI scores, which quantify the molecular impact of each variant. Access is available through a web platform, and users can query without needing an institutional affiliation, as noted in community comments.

hackernews · utiiiD · Sep 8, 14:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49611251)

**Background**: The human genome consists of DNA sequences made up of four nucleotide bases (A, T, C, G). Single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) are changes in a single base pair, and they can influence gene function and contribute to diseases. AlphaGenome Atlas uses a deep learning model to predict the effects of all possible SNVs, building on advances in AI for genomics, such as AlphaFold for protein structure.
