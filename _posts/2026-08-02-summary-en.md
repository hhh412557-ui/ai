## [OpenAI CEO Retweets Claim: Astra Model Solves 10 Major Math Problems](https://x.com/sama/status/2083553085993951362) ⭐️ 9.0/10

OpenAI's CEO Sam Altman retweeted a claim that an internal version of Astra, OpenAI's next major model family, solved 10 major open problems in mathematics, quantum complexity, and theoretical computer science. The results were released with Lean 4 formalizations and a paper, with each problem reportedly costing less than $2,000 at GPT-5.6 Sol token prices. This is significant because it suggests AI models are approaching the ability to make genuine research breakthroughs in mathematics and theoretical computer science, potentially accelerating scientific discovery. It could shift the role of mathematicians toward more creative work, as envisioned by Terence Tao's 'big mathematics' concept. The results cover long-standing open problems, including new circuit lower bounds for computing the permanent, and are accompanied by Lean 4 formalizations in the openai/ten-proofs repository, a paper, and an LLM-generated PDF reconstructing the proof process. However, the tweet is a retweet and lacks details, and no information is provided on how many problems were attempted without success.

**Background**: Astra is OpenAI's upcoming model family designed for long-running tasks, reportedly allowing multiple agents to collaborate for hours or days. Lean is a proof assistant and functional programming language used for formal verification, ensuring mathematical proofs are correct. The claim builds on recent AI achievements in mathematics, such as Anthropic's Claude discovering cryptographic weaknesses, and has sparked discussions about AI's role in mathematics.

## [ByteDance Unveils Seedance 2.5 with Flexible Referencing](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/one-take-creation-flexible-referencing-introducing-seedance-2-5) ⭐️ 8.0/10

ByteDance announced Seedance 2.5, a new AI video generation model that introduces native 30-second single-segment generation, up to 50 joined inputs, and region-level frame editing. The model aims to improve quality and offer more controllable editing compared to previous versions. Seedance 2.5 represents a significant step in AI video generation, offering longer and more controllable outputs that could appeal to filmmakers and content creators. Its release intensifies competition in the rapidly evolving AI video market, especially against open-weight models like MiniMax H3. Seedance 2.5 supports up to 50 joined inputs across images and videos, and includes region-level frame editing for finer control. The model is positioned as the first with native 30-second one-shot generation, though community members note a focus on action/high-effect shots rather than dialogue-driven scenes.

**Background**: Seedance is ByteDance's AI video generation model series, with earlier versions like Seedance 1.0 supporting multi-shot generation from text and images at 1080p. The new Seedance 2.5 builds on this by extending generation length and input flexibility, aiming to meet diverse creative needs. The model is available through various third-party platforms and APIs, as ByteDance does not appear to offer a standalone consumer app.

## [Diátaxis Framework Gains Traction for Structuring Technical Documentation](https://diataxis.fr/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Diátaxis, a framework for organizing technical documentation into four modes (tutorials, how-to guides, reference, and explanation), has gained significant community attention with a score of 8.0/10 and 241 points on Hacker News. The discussion highlights its practical benefits and challenges, and the author is actively working on translating the framework into multiple languages. This framework provides a systematic approach to documentation, which is often neglected in software engineering, potentially improving clarity and maintainability for both writers and users. Its growing adoption, as seen in community discussions and testimonials, indicates a shift towards more structured documentation practices in the industry. The framework categorizes documentation into four distinct types, each serving a different user need: tutorials for learning, how-to guides for solving specific problems, reference for information lookup, and explanation for understanding. The author, Daniele Procida, is working on translations, with an in-progress version available on Read the Docs.

hackernews · ryanseys · Aug 1, 20:33 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49138188)

**Background**: Diátaxis is a widely-adopted, pragmatic approach to documentation, as noted on its official site. It was introduced to provide a clear structure for technical writing, and has been adopted by organizations like Canonical for their documentation strategy. The framework helps writers decide what type of content to create and how to organize it, addressing common issues like duplication and inconsistency.
