## [AMD Acquires Taalas to Hardwire AI Models into Silicon](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/08/06/amd-acquires-ai-chip-startup-taalas-to-boost-inference-performance-by-etching-models-into-silicon/5284344) ⭐️ 8.0/10

AMD announced the acquisition of AI chip startup Taalas to enhance inference performance by etching AI models directly into silicon. The deal was revealed on August 6, 2026, through AMD's press release. This acquisition could significantly boost AMD's competitiveness in the AI inference market, offering performance gains that may rival or surpass existing GPUs. It also signals a broader industry trend toward specialized, model-specific hardware, potentially reshaping how AI models are deployed and updated. Taalas, a Toronto-based startup, has developed a chip that physically etches an AI model onto transistors, achieving 17,000 tokens per second on Llama 3.1 8B, nearly 10x faster than NVIDIA's H200. However, this approach sacrifices flexibility, as the chip is locked to a specific model architecture after fabrication.

**Background**: AI inference performance is critical for deploying large language models efficiently. Traditional GPUs like NVIDIA's H200 offer general-purpose flexibility but may not be optimal for specific models. Etching models into silicon creates a fixed-function pipeline, trading flexibility for speed and efficiency. This approach is part of a broader trend toward specialized AI hardware, with companies like Google already using custom TPUs.

## [Datasette 1.0a38 fixes SQL injection in mixed public/private table setups](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/6/datasette/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Datasette 1.0a38, released on August 6, 2026, fixes a SQL injection vulnerability that affects instances serving a mixture of public and private tables in the same database. The fix is also available in Datasette 0.65.3. This security fix is critical for Datasette users who expose both public and private tables, as the vulnerability could allow unauthorized read-only access to private data. It underscores the importance of promptly updating and following security recommendations for data publishing tools. The vulnerability allowed users with access to any public table to execute SQL injection attacks despite the execute-sql permission being disabled, granting read-only access to private tables. Administrators are advised to disable the execute-sql permission on affected databases to mitigate the risk.

rss · Simon Willison · Aug 6, 18:24

**Background**: Datasette is an open-source Python tool that turns SQLite databases into interactive websites and REST APIs, widely used for data publishing and exploration. The Datasette permissions system allows administrators to control access to tables, but this vulnerability bypassed those controls when public and private tables coexisted in the same database.
